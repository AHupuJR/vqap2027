About the workshop

Quality that informs a decision

Quality assessment of images and videos is most valuable when it informs a decision: tuning a camera, optimizing compression and streaming, releasing a generative model, or filtering content at scale. Yet quality depends on the application, the content and the decision at hand, while most research still targets general-purpose metrics evaluated on a few benchmarks.

The Quality Assessment in Practice (QAP) workshop advances application-driven assessment: methods designed for specific tasks, validated under realistic conditions and deployed in real systems. Through invited talks, contributed papers and a panel, we explore how to build, validate and deploy trustworthy quality assessment.

A central question: what does it take for a quality metric to be trusted when it drives a real decision?

Scope

Topics of interest

Submissions should connect a quality assessment method to a concrete application, decision, system, or deployment constraint. Topics include, but are not limited to:

1

Perceptually consistent assessment

Predictions that agree with human judgment, including fine-grained differences in high-fidelity content and consistency across content, devices, and viewing conditions.

2

Generative and AI-enhanced content

Assessment of restored, edited, and generated images and videos, including consistency with the user's intent and, for videos, temporal consistency of identities and objects and consistency with physical rules.

3

Quality models as optimization targets

Using quality models as losses, rewards, and data filters, and keeping them robust when systems are optimized against them.

4

Multimodal and explainable assessment

Vision-language models as quality judges, interpretable quality reports, and benchmarks for quality assessment.

5

Assessment in deployment

Efficient and reliable quality assessment across smartphone photography, streaming, AR/VR, relighting, 3D reconstruction and novel-view synthesis, and machine-oriented tasks.

We also welcome related perspectives on how quality assessment can be built, validated, and deployed for any application where visual quality matters. Deployment reports, including negative results, are welcome when they provide evidence of where, why, and under what conditions a quality assessment method succeeds or fails.

Motivation

Four gaps between benchmarks and practice

A metric that performs well on a general-purpose benchmark may be inadequate for a specific task: it may fail to distinguish near-identical candidates, penalize plausible restorations, overlook semantically incorrect or physically implausible generations, or prove too costly for real-time deployment.

GapWhat benchmark-based evaluation emphasizesWhat a specific application requires
DecisionCorrelation with subjective quality scores aggregated over a test setFine-grained discrimination between similar candidates; reliable thresholds for acceptance and rejection; consistent decisions across content, devices, and viewing conditions
ContentGeneral-purpose perceptual quality across diverse images and videosTask-specific criteria: fidelity and naturalness in restoration; realism, semantic consistency, and consistency with user intent in generation; temporal and physical consistency in generated videos; authentic capture and processing artifacts; intended effects such as film grain, bokeh, and stylization; HDR or ultra-high-resolution content
RolePassive scoring of a fixed test setIntegration into operational pipelines as a filter, user or encoder feedback signal, or training loss or reward, with robustness to optimization against the metric
CostPredictive accuracy as the primary criterionPractical computational and memory budgets, low latency, calibrated confidence, and interpretable outputs that support actionable decisions
Call for Papers

Submit your work

We invite submissions to two non-archival tracks. Accepted work will not appear in the CVPR 2027 Workshops proceedings, so authors can publish expanded versions elsewhere.

Track 1

Published Work

Papers recently accepted at peer-reviewed venues. Submissions must cite the original publication, and presenting them must comply with the original publisher's copyright terms.

Track 2

Novel Work

Unpublished contributions, as full papers (up to 8 pages, excluding references) or extended abstracts (up to 2 pages).

  • Submission: via OpenReview (link will be posted here when the call opens).
  • Presentation: four accepted papers will be presented as spotlight talks during the workshop.
  • Review criteria: novelty and technical rigor, and how clearly each submission connects its quality assessment method to the decision, system, or practical constraint it serves.
  • Ethics: authors are encouraged to report informed-consent procedures and participant compensation for subjective studies, and to respect the licenses, privacy, and usage restrictions of the images they use.
